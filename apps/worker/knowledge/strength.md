# Strength Gain

## What strength means

Strength is the ability to produce force.

Getting stronger means becoming able to:

Lift more weight
Perform harder bodyweight exercises
Perform more controlled repetitions
Maintain better control during movement

Strength and muscle size are related but are not exactly the same.

## How to become stronger

The main principle is progressive overload.

Progressive overload means gradually asking the body to do more over time.

Progress can come from:

More weight
More repetitions
More difficult exercise variations
Better range of motion
Better control
More challenging positions

Progress should be gradual.

Trying to increase everything at once is unnecessary.

## Strength training repetitions

Lower repetition ranges with challenging resistance are commonly useful for strength.

However, beginners can become stronger across a wide range of repetitions.

Technique should be more important than chasing a number.

## Rest between hard sets

Strength work usually benefits from longer rest periods than light cardio.

A person should rest enough to perform the next set with good technique.

If the next set becomes much worse because the person is still exhausted, more rest may be useful.

## Strength exercises

Useful movements include:

Lower body:

Squats
Split squats
Lunges
Deadlift variations
Hip thrusts

Upper body:

Push-ups
Bench press
Overhead press
Rows
Pull-ups

Core:

Planks
Side planks
Dead bugs
Loaded carries when equipment is available

## Beginner strength training

Beginners should focus on:

Learning the movement
Using manageable difficulty
Maintaining good form
Training consistently
Gradually increasing difficulty

A beginner does not need to train to complete exhaustion.

## Strength without equipment

Bodyweight progression can be used.

For push-ups:

Wall push-up
Incline push-up
Knee push-up
Standard push-up
Close-grip push-up
Decline push-up
Other harder variations

For legs:

Squat
Pause squat
Split squat
Reverse lunge
Bulgarian split squat
Single-leg variations

## Strength plateaus

If progress stops:

Check technique
Check recovery
Check sleep
Check whether training is consistent
Avoid increasing difficulty too quickly
Change the exercise variation if needed
Allow enough recovery

A plateau does not always mean the person needs more training.
