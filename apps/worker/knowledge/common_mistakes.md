# Common Exercise and Training Mistakes

## Trying to progress too quickly

Problem:
Increasing repetitions or difficulty too quickly can make recovery harder and technique worse.

Better approach:
Increase difficulty gradually.

## Chasing repetitions instead of form

Problem:
Doing more repetitions with poor technique does not necessarily mean better performance.

Better approach:
Prioritize controlled repetitions.

## Training to exhaustion every time

Problem:
Every workout does not need to be maximal.

Better approach:
Use challenging but manageable training.

## Ignoring recovery

Problem:
Training without enough recovery can reduce performance.

Better approach:
Allow enough rest, food, hydration, and sleep.

## Doing only cardio to become lean

Problem:
Cardio can help with energy expenditure and fitness, but removing strength training can make muscle retention harder.

Better approach:
Combine resistance training with appropriate cardio and nutrition.

## Eating far too little

Problem:
Very aggressive dieting can cause tiredness and may make muscle and strength retention harder.

Better approach:
Use a moderate and sustainable approach.

## Avoiding carbohydrates completely

Problem:
Carbohydrates are a useful source of energy, especially for demanding exercise.

Better approach:
Choose an appropriate amount based on the person's goal and activity.

## Copying another person's diet

Problem:
Food requirements differ between people.

Better approach:
Adapt food choices to the person's needs, preferences, activity, and goal.

## Poor warm-up

Problem:
Starting intense exercise immediately can feel uncomfortable.

Better approach:
Begin with light movement and gradually increase intensity.

## Ignoring pain

Normal effort and mild temporary fatigue are different from sharp, severe, or unusual pain.

If an exercise causes significant pain, stop the exercise and seek appropriate professional advice.

## Poor exercise selection

A person does not need every exercise.

Choose movements that:

Match the goal
Can be performed safely
Can be progressed
Fit available equipment
The person can perform consistently

## Comparing performance with others

People have different:

Strength
Body structure
Experience
Recovery
Fitness levels

Compare improvements with your own previous performance.
