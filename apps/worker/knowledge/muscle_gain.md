# Muscle Gain

## What muscle gain means

Muscle gain means increasing muscle size over time.

Muscle growth is supported by:

Resistance training

Enough food
Enough protein
Recovery

Sleep

Consistency

## Resistance training

Muscles need a reason to adapt.

Useful training includes:

Squats
Lunges
Push-ups
Presses
Rows
Pull-ups
Hip hinges
Curls
Triceps exercises
Calf exercises

## Repetitions

Muscle growth can happen across a relatively wide repetition range when the exercise is challenging enough.

A practical approach is to use moderate repetitions and gradually increase difficulty.

## Sets

More training volume can support growth, but more is not always better.

If a person constantly feels exhausted or their performance is getting worse, training volume may be too high.

## Training close to failure

A set can be challenging without every set being an all-out effort.

Most training should allow the person to maintain good technique.

Stopping a set when technique is breaking down is better than forcing poor repetitions.

## Protein

Protein provides amino acids used by the body for muscle repair and growth.

Useful protein foods include:

Eggs
Milk
Yogurt
Paneer
Tofu
Soy
Lentils
Beans
Chickpeas
Fish
Chicken
Lean meat

For vegetarian diets, combining different protein-rich foods throughout the day can help.

## Calories

Muscle gain can be easier when the person has enough energy available.

A small calorie surplus may support muscle gain, but very large surpluses are not necessary.

Rapid weight gain can increase unnecessary fat gain.

## Sleep

Sleep supports recovery and physical performance.

A consistent sleep schedule is useful.

## Muscle gain and cardio

Cardio does not automatically prevent muscle growth.

A person can combine strength training and cardio.

However, very large amounts of intense cardio combined with insufficient food and recovery can make muscle gain harder.
