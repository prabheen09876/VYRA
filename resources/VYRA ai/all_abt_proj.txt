Everything below was measured, not assumed. Where a number is environment-

================================================================================
1. PROFILE FIRST
================================================================================
Backend stage profile (480x270 input, per frame):

    jpeg decode            0.42 ms
    YOLO inference        24.47 ms      <- 97% of the total
    One Euro smoothing     0.11 ms
    validation             0.15 ms
    angle extraction       0.04 ms
    -------------------------------
    process_frame         25.10 ms

Inference is effectively the entire backend. Micro-optimising the rest would
have bought nothing, so the work went into inference and into the frontend.

Frontend per-frame cost, measured in-browser:

    drawImage              0.5 ms
    createImageBitmap      0.6 ms
    canvas.toBlob (JPEG)  42.8 ms      <- larger than the whole backend
    bone solve, 10 seg    <0.05 ms
    three.js render        0.7 ms

The real frontend bottleneck was JPEG encoding on the main thread, where it
competed directly with the render loop.

================================================================================
2. WHAT WAS CHANGED, AND WHY
================================================================================

Inference resolution
  Swept capture width against inference resolution, scored against a 640px
  native reference:

    imgsz  latency  detection rate  median keypoint error
     448   35.9 ms       100%             18.2 px
     384   29.5 ms        94%             20.7 px
     320   21.4 ms        94%             27.4 px

  Capture width barely moved the error; inference resolution dominated it.
  Settled on imgsz=384 and matched the upload width to it - sending more
  pixels than the model consumes was pure waste.

ONNX thread pool
  ONNX Runtime defaults to the physical core count. Measured at 384px:
  2 threads 26.7 ms, 4 threads 23.0 ms, 6 threads 19.0 ms, 8 threads 20.3 ms.
  Pinned to (logical cores - 2), leaving headroom for the event loop and JPEG
  decode. Fails soft: if the ultralytics internals move, the original session
  keeps working.

  Backend result: 25.10 ms -> 20.43 ms per frame (40 -> 49 FPS).

Off-thread JPEG encode
  The main thread now only calls createImageBitmap (~0.6 ms, transferable with
  no copy) and hands the bitmap to a worker that owns an OffscreenCanvas.

  Main-thread cost per frame: 19.3 ms -> 0.7 ms (p95 28.5 -> 1.0 ms).

  Note: 42.8 ms for toBlob is inflated by this headless, software-rendered
  test browser. Real hardware encodes far faster. The architectural point
  stands either way - encoding does not belong on the thread that renders.

================================================================================
3. FAST-MOTION REP DETECTION
================================================================================
Measured failure of the previous threshold-and-confirm logic at 30 FPS:

    2.00 s/rep -> 6 of 6      0.45 s/rep -> 0 of 6
    1.00 s/rep -> 5 of 6      0.35 s/rep -> 0 of 6
    0.60 s/rep -> 5 of 6

The cause was not threshold sensitivity. The smoothing EMA is a low-pass
filter, so faster reps lose amplitude: at 0.45 s/rep the smoothed elbow angle
bottomed out at 87 deg instead of 72, and the top was pulled below the 155 deg
extension gate. The rep happened; the filtered signal never reached either gate.

Two changes, neither of which is "lower the threshold":

  1. Phase is driven by direction reversal. A reversal is declared once the
     angle retreats REVERSAL_DELTA (10 deg) from the running extreme. That is
     an amplitude test, so it behaves identically at any frame rate or speed,
     and fires on the first sample after the turn rather than after N
     confirmations.

  2. Rep extrema are measured on the RAW angle. Smoothing still decides
     direction, but depth and extension are judged on unfiltered samples, so
     fast reps keep their true amplitude.

Result - push-ups, 6 of 6 counted at every speed tested:

    speed        20 FPS  30 FPS  50 FPS
    2.00 s/rep     6       6       6
    1.00 s/rep     6       6       6
    0.60 s/rep     6       6       6
    0.45 s/rep     6       6       6
    0.35 s/rep     6       6       6
    0.25 s/rep     6       6       6

And still rejects what it should:
    half-depth reps     0 counted, 10 flagged as no-reps
    jitter at the top   0 counted
    sagging body        0 counted
    shallow squats      0 counted, 10 flagged

Squats use the same machine on knee angle: 6 of 6 from 2.0 s down to 0.4 s.

Exercise modes are separate detector instances selected by exerciseMode. Test
9 in the Python suite drives one and asserts the other's counter never moves.

================================================================================
4. UNIVERSAL RETARGETING
================================================================================
Motion is expressed once as universal segments (SPINE, NECK, UPPER_ARM_L/R,
FOREARM_L/R, THIGH_L/R, SHIN_L/R). Each character supplies a bone map; the
solver is rest-relative, so it measures each segment's own rest direction on
load and rotates relative to that. That is why rigs as different as Mixamo
(66 bones), a 452-bone anime rig and a 29-bone rig need no hand-authored
rotation offsets.

Verified: all four rigged characters bind 10/10 segments, every segment is
driven by the pose, and the forearm sweep spread across all four rigs is
0.00 deg from identical input - the motion layer is genuinely character-
independent.

A measurement trap worth recording: a first pass appeared to show Luffy's
thighs static. They were not. SPINE solves first and rotates the whole lower
body, so a child's LOCAL quaternion barely changes even as the limb clearly
moves in world space. The tests now measure world-space segment directions.

================================================================================
5. CAMERA ORIENTATION
================================================================================
Mirroring now has exactly one owner (src/pose/CameraTransform.js) and is
presentation-only:

  - drawImage ignores CSS transforms, so the frame sent for inference is always
    the raw sensor image.
  - YOLO labels keypoints anatomically, so `left_shoulder` is the person's own
    left however the image is flipped.
  - Nothing downstream flips coordinates.

Verified: joint angle identical mirrored (79.70 vs 79.70 deg), orientation
class and animatability survive mirroring, elbow angle unchanged by flip
(114.78 vs 114.78 deg), anatomical left/right stay on the correct character
side, and rep count is identical mirrored vs unmirrored (6 vs 6).

================================================================================
6. CLEAN CAMERA FEED
================================================================================
No landmark dots, skeleton lines or boxes are drawn on the user's camera.
Detection is unaffected - only the drawing is suppressed. The DEV HUD button
toggles DEBUG_TRACKING, which re-enables the overlay plus FPS, latency, angles
and confidence. Default is off.

================================================================================
7. MEASURED RESULTS
================================================================================
Backend, isolated:
    process_frame            20.43 ms median  (49 FPS capability)
    server inference         24.9 ms median, 28.2 ms p95
    round trip send->reply   26.5 ms median   (network + queue = ~1.6 ms)
    upstream                 ~10 KB/frame
    downstream               1221 B/frame  (36 KB/s at 30 FPS)

Frontend, in-browser:
    capture + encode (main)   0.7 ms median, 1.0 ms p95
    bone solve, 10 segments  <0.05 ms
    three.js render           0.7 ms

Caveat: when the dev server, headless browser and video decode all share this
8-core machine, measured inference rises to ~70 ms. The 24.9 ms figure is the
clean one; the contended figure is an artefact of testing everything on one box.

Render work reduced structurally, with no change to geometry or textures:
pixel ratio capped at 1.5, render-on-demand when idle, per-segment deadband so
unchanged bones are not re-solved, skinned meshes exempt from frustum culling
(their rest-pose bounds would clip limbs mid-rep), and full GPU disposal on
character swap - previously every swap leaked a model.

================================================================================
9. KNOWN LIMITS
================================================================================
- Four supplied characters (Base_Female, goku, Female_Sakura, Female_Mikasa)
  have no skeleton in the GLB and cannot be posed. They are excluded from the
  catalog rather than shipped as characters that silently refuse to move.
- COCO-17 has no hand or toe landmarks, so hands and feet inherit their parent
  bone rather than being driven. Head, neck, spine, arms and legs are driven.
- Depth is synthesised, not measured. Movement toward or away from the camera
  is not reproduced on the character.
- Inference is CPU-only here (torch reports CUDA unavailable). A CUDA or
  DirectML execution provider would add significant headroom.
- Rep and gamification state is global to the server process, so it assumes a
  single trainee; a new connection resets the session.
- requestAnimationFrame is throttled in a hidden tab, so the browser
  measurements above were taken by driving the loops on a timer. Real
  foreground rAF scheduling was not measurable in this headless environment.
