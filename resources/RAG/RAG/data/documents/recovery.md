Recovery and Rest
Why recovery matters

The body adapts to training during recovery.

Recovery supports:

Muscle repair
Strength improvement
Energy
Training performance
Consistency
Sleep

Good sleep is important for physical performance and recovery.

Most adults should aim for roughly 7–9 hours of sleep, although individual needs can vary.

Rest days

A person does not need to train hard every day.

Rest days can include:

Complete rest
Walking
Light mobility
Easy activity
Muscle soreness

Mild muscle soreness can happen after unfamiliar or harder exercise.

Soreness does not automatically mean a workout was better.

A person should not use severe soreness as a target.

Hydration

Drink water regularly.

Fluid needs increase with:

Heat
Sweating
Long workouts
Running
Sports
Food and recovery

Recovery requires enough energy and nutrients.

Useful foods include:

Protein-rich foods
Carbohydrates
Fruits
Vegetables
Healthy fats
Training fatigue

Signs that training may need adjustment:

Performance consistently getting worse
Constant tiredness
Poor sleep
Persistent soreness
Loss of motivation
Difficulty completing normal workouts

The person may benefit from reducing training difficulty or taking additional recovery.

Warm-up

A warm-up prepares the body for activity.

A simple warm-up can include:

Light walking
Easy jogging
Dynamic movement
Movement-specific practice
Cool-down

A cool-down can involve:

Easy walking
Gentle movement
Gradually lowering exercise intensity

A cool-down does not need to be complicated.
