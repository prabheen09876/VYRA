Push-up Improvement
Main goal

Push-ups mainly train the chest, shoulders, and triceps while the core helps maintain body position.

Basic setup
Place hands on a stable surface.
Keep the body in a controlled straight line.
Keep the core engaged.
Lower the body under control.
Push back up.
Common mistakes
Hips dropping

The body should remain controlled instead of letting the hips sag.

Cue:
"Keep your body in one line."

Hips too high

The exercise becomes less like a standard push-up.

Cue:
"Keep your hips in line with your shoulders and legs."

Not lowering enough

Use a controlled range of motion that the person can maintain.

Elbows moving too far outward

Keep the elbows controlled rather than forcing them directly sideways.

Moving too quickly

Slow down and control both directions.

Incomplete repetition

A repetition should meet the person's chosen range-of-motion standard.

Push-up progression

Easier:

Wall push-up
High incline push-up
Lower incline push-up
Knee push-up when appropriate

Standard:
5. Regular push-up

Harder:
6. Close-grip push-up
7. Decline push-up
8. Other controlled variations

Improving push-up strength

Progress by:

Increasing repetitions
Increasing range of motion
Using a harder variation
Improving control
Reducing unnecessary rest when appropriate

Do not increase difficulty so much that technique becomes poor.

VYRA feedback

If VYRA detects:

Hips dropping → "Keep your body straight."
High hips → "Keep your hips aligned."
Shallow movement → "Use a controlled range of motion."
Poor control → "Slow the movement down."
