VYRA User Manual
What is VYRA?

VYRA is an interactive fitness application that turns exercise into an engaging experience.

The user performs exercises while VYRA observes movement and provides performance feedback.

VYRA can help users work on:

Exercise technique
Repetition consistency
Strength
Fitness
Stamina
Progress
Workout challenges
First-time use

When opening VYRA for the first time:

Allow the camera if VYRA asks for camera access.
Position yourself where your body can be clearly seen.
Make sure there is enough space to exercise.
Keep the camera stable.
Select the desired exercise or workout.
Read the exercise instructions before starting.
Start when ready.
Camera positioning

The camera should:

Have a clear view of the person
Show the relevant body parts
Remain reasonably stable
Have sufficient lighting

Avoid:

Very dark rooms
Blocking the camera
Standing too close
Standing so far away that the body becomes difficult to see
Starting a workout

Before starting:

Choose the exercise
Check the instructions
Make sure the exercise space is safe
Position yourself correctly
Start when ready
During a workout

Perform the exercise normally.

Do not intentionally move unnaturally just to increase the score.

Focus on:

Good technique
Controlled movement
Consistent repetitions
Following the feedback
Repetition count

VYRA attempts to identify completed repetitions.

A repetition may not count if the movement is incomplete or difficult for the system to identify.

Users should not rush just to increase the count.

Form score

The form score represents how closely the detected movement matches the movement rules used by VYRA.

A lower score does not mean the person is "bad" at exercise.

It means there are movement areas that can be improved.

Feedback

VYRA may provide feedback such as:

"Go a little deeper."
"Keep your body straight."
"Slow down."
"Keep your chest more upright."
"Good control."

The user should focus on one correction at a time instead of trying to change everything immediately.

Improving after feedback

Example:

VYRA says:

"Your squat depth is too shallow."

The user should:

Slow down.
Focus on controlled depth.
Repeat the movement.
Check whether the next repetitions improve.
Challenges

VYRA may turn exercise performance into challenges.

A challenge can consider:

Repetition count
Form quality
Consistency
Exercise difficulty
Previous performance

The user should focus on good exercise rather than blindly maximizing the score.

Game or battle mode

If VYRA includes a battle or competitive mode, exercise performance can influence the user's in-game results.

For example:

Good form can improve performance.
Consistent repetitions can contribute to a challenge.
Strong performance can unlock rewards or advantages.

The exact game rules depend on the current VYRA mode.

Starting a challenge

Before a challenge:

Read the objective.
Check the exercise.
Position yourself.
Start the exercise.
Follow the movement instructions.
Complete the challenge.
During a challenge

Do not sacrifice technique simply to perform more repetitions.

If the system gives form feedback, use it to improve the next repetitions.

Pausing

If a pause option is available, use it when:

You need a short rest.
You need to reposition yourself.
The camera position needs adjustment.
You need to understand the instructions.
Stopping a workout

Stop the workout if:

You feel excessively tired.
You experience significant pain.
You feel dizzy or faint.
You feel unwell.
You cannot maintain safe technique.

If serious or unusual symptoms occur, seek appropriate medical attention.

Ending a workout

After finishing:

Review the repetition count.
Review the form score.
Look at feedback.
Check improvements.
Note areas to work on next time.
Understanding progress

Do not judge progress from one workout.

Look at trends over time:

Better form
More controlled repetitions
Higher strength
Longer running duration
Better stamina
Improved consistency

How VYRA should respond to different users
Beginner

Give:

Simple instructions
Easier exercise options
Short feedback
Gradual progression

Do not overwhelm the user with too many corrections.

Intermediate user

Give:

More specific form feedback
More challenging exercise options
Progressive challenges
Performance comparisons
Advanced user

Give:

More difficult variations
More detailed performance information
More challenging goals
Greater emphasis on consistency and performance quality
Important safety rule

VYRA is a fitness guidance and entertainment tool. It should not diagnose injuries, illnesses, or medical conditions.

VYRA should not tell a user to ignore pain or medical advice.

If a user has an injury, medical condition, unusual symptoms, or is unsure whether an exercise is appropriate, they should consult an appropriate healthcare professional.

VYRA philosophy

The goal is not:

"Do the most repetitions possible."

The goal is:

"Move well, improve gradually, become stronger and fitter, and enjoy the process."

VYRA should encourage progress without encouraging unsafe behavior.
