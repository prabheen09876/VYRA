Squat Improvement
Main goal

The squat trains the legs and hips and also requires core and body control.

Basic squat setup
Stand with feet in a comfortable position.
Keep the feet stable.
Keep the body balanced.
Begin the movement by bending the hips and knees.
Lower under control.
Stand back up smoothly.
Depth

Depth depends on the person's mobility, body structure, experience, and goal.

A person should use a range of motion they can control safely.

VYRA should not automatically treat one exact knee angle as perfect for every person.

Common squat problems
Insufficient depth

Possible causes:

Lack of confidence
Limited mobility
Weakness
Moving too quickly

Useful cue:
"Lower your hips with control."

Excessive forward lean

Useful cue:
"Keep your chest more upright."

The person should not force an unnatural upright position.

Knees moving inward

The person should focus on controlled knee tracking and stable feet.

Heels lifting

Possible causes include:

Foot position
Mobility limitations
Balance problems

The person should work on controlled movement rather than forcing depth.

Moving too quickly

Slow down enough to control the descent and ascent.

Improving squat strength

Useful progressions:

Bodyweight squat
Pause squat
Tempo squat
Split squat
Reverse lunge
Bulgarian split squat
Loaded squat when appropriate
Squat practice

A beginner can practice:

Consistent stance
Controlled descent
Controlled ascent
Stable feet
Comfortable depth

Quality is more important than rapidly increasing repetitions.

VYRA feedback

If VYRA detects:

Shallow depth → encourage controlled deeper movement if appropriate
Forward lean → encourage better torso control
Knee collapse → encourage controlled knee tracking
Incomplete repetition → explain that the movement was not fully completed

Feedback should be short and easy to understand.
